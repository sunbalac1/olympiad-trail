The standard Grade 6 Olympiad syllabus

1. Mathematics Olympiad (e.g., IMO, NSTSE) 
The math curriculum demands mastery over calculation shortcuts, properties, and conceptual depth. [1, 7]  
Number System & Arithmetic • Knowing Our Numbers: Large numbers up to 8 digits, estimation, place value, Roman numerals. 
• Whole Numbers: Properties of numbers (closure, commutative, associative, distributive), number line representation. 
• Playing with Numbers: Factors and multiples, prime/composite numbers, divisibility rules, HCF and LCM word problems. 
• Integers: Concept of negative numbers, absolute value, operations on integers. 
• Fractions & Decimals: Types of fractions, conversion, operations, word problems involving decimal fractions. 
• Ratio and Proportion: Concept of ratio, unitary method, direct proportions. 
• Commercial Math: Introduction to percentages, simple profit & loss calculations. [1, 8, 9]  

Algebra & Geometry • Introduction to Algebra: Variable concepts, constants, forming algebraic expressions, solving basic linear equations. 
• Basic Geometrical Ideas: Points, lines, line segments, rays, intersecting and parallel lines, open/closed curves. 
• Understanding Shapes: Polygons, triangles (by sides and angles), quadrilaterals, 3D shapes (prisms, pyramids, cones). 
• Symmetry & Practical Geometry: Line of symmetry, rotational symmetry reflection, constructions using a ruler and compass (perpendicular bisectors, angles). [1, 8, 9, 10]  

Mensuration & Data Handling • Perimeter and Area: Formulas for squares, rectangles, and regular polygons; complex composite figures. 
• Data Handling: Data collection and organization, pictographs, bar graphs, interpreting complex data maps. [1, 9, 10]  

2. Science Olympiad (e.g., NSO, NSTSE) 
Science is split into three foundational branches, focusing on conceptual clarity and real-world application. [11, 12]  
Physics • Motion & Measurement: Types of motion (rectilinear, circular, periodic), history of transport, units of measurement, standard unit conversions. 
• Light, Shadows, & Reflections: Luminous vs. non-luminous objects, transparent/translucent/opaque media, pinhole camera mechanics, characteristics of reflections. 
• Electricity & Circuits: Electric cell components, open vs. closed circuits, switches, conductors, and insulators. 
• Fun with Magnets: Magnetic materials, poles of a magnet, magnetic compass tracking, demagnetization. [2, 13]  

Chemistry • Sorting & Separation of Materials: Hardness, solubility, luster, transparency, methods of separation (winnowing, sedimentation, decantation, filtration, evaporation). 
• Changes Around Us: Reversible and irreversible changes, physical vs. chemical transformations, expansion and contraction. 
• Natural Resources (Water & Air): The water cycle, composition of air, importance of oxygen, air pollution, conservation. [2, 11, 13]  

Biology • Food and Its Components: Sources of food, nutrients (carbohydrates, fats, proteins, vitamins, minerals), balanced diet, deficiency diseases. 
• Fibre to Fabric: Natural vs. synthetic fibers, plant fibers (cotton, jute), processing steps (ginning, spinning, weaving, knitting). 
• Living World & Diversity: Characteristics of living organisms, plant anatomy (roots, stem, leaves, flowers), animal body movements (joints, skeleton, gait of animals). 
• Habitats & Adaptations: Terrestrial, aquatic, and aerial environments; plant and animal survival mechanisms in extreme climates. [11, 13]  

3. Logical Reasoning & Critical Thinking 
This section appears in almost all Tier-1 Olympiads to test mental aptitude and problem-solving velocity. [3, 7]  
Verbal Reasoning • Series Completion: Number and alpha-numeric patterns. 
• Coding-Decoding: Letter shifting, substitution codes, number coding. 
• Blood Relations & Ranking: Decoding family trees, positional rankings in a row. 
• Direction Sense: Compass orientation and distance mapping. 
• Analogy & Classification: Finding logical links, identifying the "Odd One Out". [5, 14]  

Non-Verbal Reasoning • Visual Logic: Figure matrix completion, identifying embedded figures, grouping identical figures. 
• Spatial Operations: Paper folding, paper cutting, cube and dice rotations. 
• Mirror & Water Images: Lateral inversion and vertical flipping. [5, 14]  

4. English Olympiad (e.g., IEO, UIEO) 
The English framework emphasizes deep situational comprehension and advanced grammar nuance. 

• Word & Structure Knowledge: Nouns, pronouns, verbs, adverbs, adjectives, articles, prepositions, conjunctions, tenses, active/passive voice, direct/indirect narration, collocations, phrasal verbs, idioms, synonyms, and antonyms. 
• Reading Comprehension: Extracting information from text layouts like brochures, itineraries, news stories, formal/informal letters, and timetables. 
• Spoken & Written Expression: Situational communication skills (e.g., matching responses to appropriate requests, refusals, apologies, and agreements). [4, 15]

3. Logical Reasoning & Critical Thinking 
This section assesses structural organization, analytical capacities, and a flexible intellect. 

• Verbal Reasoning 

	• Alphabet Test: Arranging letters, decoding words, finding words within words. 
	• Coding-Decoding: Substituting numbers or codes for words. 
	• Ranking Test: Finding a position from left/right or top/bottom. 
	• Direction Sense Test: Navigating north, south, east, and west paths. 
	• Analogy and Classification: Grouping items or identifying matching pairs based on relationships. 
	• Blood Relations: Deciphering family structures and relationships. [1, 5]  

• Non-Verbal Reasoning 

	• Patterns: Identifying shape sequences, figure completions, and series continuation. 
	• Mirror and Water Images: Visualizing inverted configurations. 
	• Embedded Figures: Finding a smaller shape hidden inside a complex illustration. 
	• Geometrical Solids: Counting the number of faces, edges, or hidden blocks. 
	• Possible Combinations: Finding permutation arrangements of basic colors, numbers, or objects. [1, 5]  

3. Everyday Mathematics 
This section does not introduce unique topics, but re-contextualizes the core subjective syllabus into practical settings. 

• Real-Life Problem Solving: Applying arithmetic directly to shopping, sharing meals, banking, or travel. 
• Multi-Step Calculations: Combining multi-operation parameters within singular scenarios. [2, 9]  

4. English Olympiad (e.g., IEO, UIEO)
1. Grammar and Mechanics 

• Nouns: Proper, common, collective, material, abstract, countable, and uncountable. 
• Pronouns: Personal, possessive, demonstrative, and reflexive. 
• Verbs: Action verbs, helping verbs (is, am, are, was, were), and basic modal verbs (can, may, should). 
• Tenses: Present, past, and future (simple and continuous forms). 
• Adjectives: Qualitative, quantitative, demonstrative, possessive, and degrees of comparison. 
• Adverbs: Adverbs of manner, place, time, and frequency. 
• Articles: Definite (the) and indefinite (a, an) articles. 
• Prepositions: Direction, position, time, and place. 
• Conjunctions: Coordinating conjunctions (and, but, or, so, because). 
• Punctuation: Capital letters, full stops, commas, question marks, and apostrophes for possession/contraction. 
• Sentences: Subject and predicate identification, jumbled words, types of sentences (declarative, interrogative, exclamatory, imperative). 

2. Vocabulary Enhancement 

• Synonyms & Antonyms: Identifying words with similar or opposite meanings. 
• Homophones & Homographs: Words that sound the same or are spelled the same but have different meanings. 
• Collocations: Common word pairings (e.g., "make a mistake," "take a break"). 
• Idioms & Proverbs: Age-appropriate expressions and their figurative meanings. 
• One-Word Substitutions: Replacing a phrase with a single word (e.g., "a person who designs buildings" = architect). 
• Spellings: Identifying correctly and incorrectly spelled words. 
• Anagrams & Word Ladders: Word puzzles and letter rearrangements. 

3. Reading Comprehension 

• Fact Extraction: Locating direct information from short stories or essays. 
• Inference: Understanding underlying meanings or character emotions not stated directly. 
• Context Clues: Guessing the meaning of unfamiliar words using surrounding text. 
• Sequencing: Arranging events from a passage in chronological order. 
• Poetry: Comprehending short, age-appropriate poems and rhyme schemes. 

4. Spoken and Written Expression 

• Conversational English: Choosing appropriate responses for specific situations (e.g., making requests, apologizing, greeting someone). 
• Functional Language: Language used in invitations, formal notices, telephone calls, or buying items.
